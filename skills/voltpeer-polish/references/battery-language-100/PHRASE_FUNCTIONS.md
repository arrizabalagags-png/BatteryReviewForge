# Original battery sentence functions

These are independently written patterns for editing supplied facts. Bracketed slots must be filled only from the author's verified draft. The examples are synthetic, not quotations or actual experimental results. Do not use a pattern to manufacture a mechanism, a number or a citation.

| Sentence job | Original pattern | Use condition |
|---|---|---|
| Define a comparison | “We compared [A] and [B] at the same [loading/protocol] to test [supplied question].” | The comparison and intended question are already documented |
| Report capacity | “[Electrode/cell] delivered [capacity] at [current/rate] within [voltage range].” | Preserve mass/area basis and actual electrode pairing |
| Report retention | “After [N] cycles, [cell] retained [R]% of its [defined reference capacity] at [conditions].” | The reference capacity is defined; do not invent it |
| Report asymmetric cycling | “The cell was charged at [rate A] and discharged at [rate B].” | Do not compress unlike rates into one ambiguous C-rate |
| Report plating efficiency | “[Metal]||[substrate] cells showed an average CE of [value] over [range], using [current] and [plating capacity].” | Average, range and protocol are supplied |
| Report symmetric cycling | “[Metal]||[metal] cells cycled for [time] at [current] and [areal capacity].” | State hours as hours; do not infer full-cell life |
| State a mass boundary | “The [specific energy] was calculated using [included masses], including/excluding [explicit components].” | Match the original calculation boundary exactly |
| State a temperature boundary | “The improvement persisted from [T1] to [T2] under [protocol].” | Same statement supported across that range; otherwise split conditions |
| Report a spectrum | “The [assigned band/peak] shifted from [position A] to [position B] after [perturbation].” | Keep observation separate from causal interpretation |
| Introduce an assignment | “This shift is consistent with [supplied coordination interpretation].” | A compatible but non-unique interpretation is appropriate |
| State a fit result | “Fitting the spectra with [model] gave [parameter] under [conditions].” | Do not present fitted quantities as direct measurements |
| State a calculation | “Within [model], [path] had a calculated barrier of [value].” | Process and reference states are known; binding energy is not a substitute |
| Link complementary probes | “Together, [probe A] and [probe B] support [bounded interpretation].” | Each probe is actually supplied and addresses the proposed relation |
| Keep a rival explanation | “The change may also reflect [alternative], which [current test] does not resolve.” | The author has supplied a credible unresolved alternative |
| Explain a conditional mechanism | “Under [tested regime], [result] supports [mechanism].” | Do not remove the condition to strengthen the claim |
| Preserve a negative result | “No [feature] was detected within [sampling/detection conditions].” | Absence of detection differs from proven absence |
| Preserve a failed regime | “The benefit diminished at [condition], limiting [claim] to [tested regime].” | The failed or untested regime changes interpretation |
| Distinguish cell configurations | “[Half-cell] results support [local claim]; [full-cell inference] requires testing with [actual paired electrodes].” | Do not present a future test as completed work |
| Bound practical relevance | “The result supports [specific capability] at [loading, electrolyte and inventory conditions].” | Avoid turning a laboratory capability into a deployment guarantee |
| Compare literature | “Relative to [cited study], the present work extends [finding] to [new verified condition].” | The relationship and cited source are verified |
| State non-comparability | “The reported values use different [mass bases/protocols], so they do not support a direct ranking.” | Use when the supplied comparison actually has this mismatch |
| Synthesize a review | “Across the supplied studies, [relation] recurs under [shared condition], while [difference] remains unresolved.” | Do not invent consensus or pool incomparable values |
| Make a limitation consequential | “Because [condition] was not tested, the data do not establish [specific extension].” | Name the claim affected, rather than a generic limitation |
| Propose a discriminating test | “A comparison at [controlled variable] could distinguish [A] from [B].” | This is a proposed test, not an author promise or completed experiment |
| Keep flow-cell efficiencies separate | “The flow cell returned [CE] of charge and [EE] of electrical energy under [protocol].” | Preserve the charge/energy distinction and system boundary |
| Keep degradation time bases separate | “Capacity declined by [rate] per [day/hour/cycle] during [interval].” | Do not convert time bases without a verified calculation |
| State electron utilization | “The cell accessed [fraction] of the [defined electron/theoretical capacity] under [protocol].” | Molecular electron count does not imply complete utilization |
| Clarify a source conflict | “The draft assigns [value] to [metric A], whereas [source location] assigns it to [metric B]; this needs verification.” | A factual discrepancy should be visible in notes, not silently repaired |

Choose wording by function and evidence strength. “Showed” can report a measured comparison; “supports” can introduce a favoured explanation; “may” can mark an untested extension. Neither a stronger verb nor an extra hedge can replace missing evidence. Place the citation immediately with the claim it supports, and preserve the original citation key.
