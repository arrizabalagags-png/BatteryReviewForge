# Research-article argument and outline

Use this route when the paper reports the author's research. Retain the main Skill's separate Review/Perspective route for literature synthesis.

## Resolve the immediate planning question

Use the supplied manuscript, figure set, methods, verified results and author notes. Confirm what the user wants now: a whole outline, a figure-led Results order, the logic of one section, or a recovery of a draft that has drifted. Do not make the author fill an unrelated project form for a small edit.

Record a short brief in the user's project. It should distinguish the author's approved interpretation from an AI-proposed organization. An AI may arrange evidence and suggest alternative phrasings; the author must decide the central scientific argument.

## Build an evidence-led plan

1. State the research question and the tested chemistry/configuration/conditions.
2. Inventory existing figures and results with source, units, denominator and what was actually measured. Mark planned or unmeasured work separately.
3. Propose a central claim no stronger than the evidence. List the strongest plausible alternative explanation or control that would change it.
4. Arrange subsections so each answers one question and owns a result or display item. Link observations to interpretations without treating correlation or a single spectrum as proof of a complete mechanism.
5. Allocate supporting literature to context and comparison. Record abstract-only versus full-text inspection; do not assert an unverified novelty gap.
6. End with limits, unanswered questions and a small actionable material list. A missing result is an evidence task, not a prompt to write it as completed.

## Output a useful outline

| Field | What to provide |
|---|---|
| Section / subsection | A descriptive working title |
| Reader question | The one question this section answers |
| Claim | Author-approved or explicitly proposed wording |
| Evidence | Existing result/figure/source; uncertainty and counterevidence when relevant |
| Status | Verified from provided material, needs source inspection, needs author decision, or future experiment |
| Handoff | How it leads to the next section |

Do not require a fixed number of sections, panels or experiments. Retain viable existing decisions. If information is insufficient, deliver a provisional outline with its dependencies, not a fake completed research plan.

For battery comparisons, retain half-cell/full-cell boundaries, loading/current/temperature/voltage window, cycle baseline and energy/capacity denominator. Never fill missing values from a neighbouring paper or a teaching example.

Deliver the outline or revised argument map with a concise reason for major changes and the next required material. Keep detailed provenance and recovery in `.voltpeer/`; do not bury the usable plan under execution logs.
